# Uncertainties, corrections and fact-checking

Show what is not yet known instead of turning search snippets into firm conclusions.

## High-priority checks

### Current 2026 F1 statistics
The 2026 season is ongoing. Points, position, starts, best finish, retirements and penalties must be checked against official standings and event results on the date used.
- [2026 driver standings](https://www.formula1.com/en/results/2026/drivers)
- [2026 race results](https://www.formula1.com/en/results/2026/races)
- [FIA documents](https://www.fia.com/documents)

### F4 podium count
Secondary results tables and narrative summaries are not consistent in how 2020 Italian F4 podiums are counted. Reconcile against official classifications instead of silently choosing a number.

### Historical “first” claims
Bortoleto is described as the first Brazilian FIA F3 champion; his Monza 2024 F2 comeback is reported as a last-to-first record. Preserve the exact scope and check official start position and record category before repeating “first ever”.

### Driver style and private telemetry
Public results support cautious observations but not detailed claims about braking traces, tyre temperatures, internal engineering feedback or private simulator performance.

### Sponsors
A historical sponsor is not necessarily current. Distinguish personal sponsor, team partner, family business and junior programme; verify status with dated official announcements.

### Personal life
Do not add rumours or private information merely because it appears in social posts or aggregator pages.

## Common error traps
- Confusing entries with starts or classified finishes.
- Combining sprint and Grand Prix statistics without saying so.
- Treating a team partner as a personal sponsor.
- Repeating a dated 2026 standings number as current.
- Using a headline without reading the linked article or steward decision.
- Treating fan analysis as fact.
